MASTER'S THESIS · kSZ · 3D CNN

Reconstructing Cosmic Velocities.

Can a neural network recover galaxy peculiar velocities from large-scale matter structure—and remain trustworthy when the simulation, resolution and underlying realization change?

IIT IndoreSupervisor: Dr. Priyanka SinghMay 2026 — PresentCurrent research

ON THIS PAGE

MotivationResearch questionPipelineRobustnessCurrent status

01 / MOTIVATION

Using motion to read the growth of structure.

The kinetic Sunyaev–Zel'dovich effect is sensitive to the line-of-sight motion of ionized matter. As future measurements become increasingly precise, velocity information can become a powerful route into structure growth and the distribution of matter in the Universe.

ΔTkSZ / TCMB ∝ − ∫ ne v∥ dl

This thesis approaches the problem from the reconstruction side: learning the relationship between the matter-density field and halo peculiar velocities, then asking whether that learned mapping remains stable outside the exact simulation setup used for training.

02 / RESEARCH QUESTION

Accuracy is not enough if the model only understands one simulation.

The central scientific and methodological question is whether a 3D CNN can reconstruct peculiar velocity fields while maintaining useful performance across simulations with different effective resolutions and independently resolved volumes.

Current focus: cross-simulation resolution robustness and controlled generalization tests using CAMELS-TNG and public IllustrisTNG simulations.

04 / ROBUSTNESS

Testing what the network actually learned.

A model can look excellent on data that resemble its training set. The more interesting test is whether the reconstruction survives a controlled shift in simulation volume and resolution. That makes robustness itself part of the scientific result, not merely a software detail.

The pipeline, training framework and robustness study are actively being developed. Public plots, repository links and manuscript information will be added only when they are ready to share.
