WORKING NOTE · CMB / kSZ

What does the kSZ effect actually let us reconstruct?

A compact intuition-first note on why the motion of free electrons leaves a temperature imprint on the CMB—and why that imprint can become a route to cosmic velocity information.

Draft status: working note~6 min outlineWill be revised as the thesis develops
This is intentionally marked as a working draft, not a finished publication or research result.

The basic idea

CMB photons can Thomson-scatter from free electrons that have a bulk peculiar velocity relative to the cosmic rest frame. The resulting Doppler shift produces the kinetic Sunyaev–Zel'dovich signal. Schematically, the temperature fluctuation depends on the free-electron density weighted by the line-of-sight velocity.

ΔTkSZ / TCMB ∝ − ∫ ne v∥ dl

Why this is interesting

The signal is faint, but it contains information that is difficult to obtain from density alone: coherent motion. That makes kSZ-related measurements potentially valuable for understanding the growth of large-scale structure and the velocity field associated with it.

Where machine learning enters

My thesis explores a related reconstruction problem: can a 3D CNN infer halo peculiar velocities from the surrounding matter-density field, and can that mapping remain robust when the underlying simulation setup changes?

The question I care about

A high test-set score is not enough if the network only works for one particular simulation or resolution. The more interesting question is whether the reconstructed velocity information is stable across independently resolved volumes.
